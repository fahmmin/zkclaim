# zkClaim: the judge's seat

Decision for Hackasol 5.0, W3-01, 23 November 2026: **build a wallet-free settlement receipt first.** Let a judge check a payout without giving them the patient's chart or asking them to trust the hospital dashboard. If a contract change clears a real deployment rehearsal, add a second decision the judge can make as the patient: **withdraw permission before the money moves**.

## What you can show today

| Judge's question | Evidence already in the repo |
| --- | --- |
| "If the limit is private, why can't the hospital just invent a bigger one?" | The insurer registers a commitment to the actual terms. The claim circuit recomputes it from the private terms and rejects an unissued commitment. The existing **Doctor the policy card** challenge returns `Unknown policy`; it is not just client-side form validation. [Contract](../contract/src/zkclaim.compact#L278-L291), [passing test](../contract/src/test/zkclaim.test.ts#L135-L139), [Tamper Lab scenario](../ui/src/zk/tamper.ts#L145-L157). |
| "Can two individually valid bills exceed one policy limit?" | Settlement checks cumulative spend, not just this invoice. A test pays the first bill and refuses the second. [Contract](../contract/src/zkclaim.compact#L303-L322), [test](../contract/src/test/zkclaim.test.ts#L167-L177). This does not establish that a real-world visit happened or that it was never re-entered as another record. |
| "Is revoke just hiding a button?" | For record sharing, the patient re-encrypts the current record under new keys and re-wraps keys for remaining grantees. An old key fails on the new ciphertext. It cannot erase a copy already read. [Revoke flow](../ui/src/zk/demo.ts#L567-L583), [key-rotation test](../records/src/test/record.test.ts#L110-L120). This is **not** withdrawal of claim consent. |

The supplied brief (printed page 8) makes ZK claims, automatic settlement, doctor permissions and three roles the baseline. Timed/role grants, logged break-glass and FHIR sharing are named bonuses. A local chain is explicitly acceptable. The page gives no numerical judging weights. A useful external benchmark is [ProofSure's August 2026 write-up](https://devfolio.co/projects/proofsure-d272): a team at a different event built private claims and documented the difficulty of binding private limits to issued terms. That's a reason to demonstrate the first row, not evidence about any Hackasol entrant. No complete W3-01 competitor list was verified.

## Ranked bets

These are stage priorities, not official scores. Effort is a rough estimate for a developer familiar with this repo, with the toolchain already working. The first two form one story; do not attempt all four.

| Rank | Addition | What the judge can personally verify | Effort / risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Wallet-free **settlement receipt** | Open it in a clean browser with no wallet; a real amount matches finalized state. Change one digit and it no longer matches. No medical fields reach that verifier. | 2-4 hours; no contract change. Best value before judging. |
| 2 | **Withdraw a pending claim authorization** | Two otherwise eligible claims: the authorized one pays; for the other, withdrawal finalizes first and the hospital cannot settle it. The paid total does not move. | 4-8 hours including fresh proving keys and a local redeploy. Best privacy/compliance addition. |
| 3 | **India claims-format export** | Validate one synthetic settlement response against the published NHCX `ClaimResponseBundle` profile. Show the actual validation report and one invalid fixture, not an integration logo. | 3-6 hours if the profile/validator are cached. Useful for an interoperability judge; not needed for the main demo. |
| 4 | **Emergency keys released only after authorization** | A separate custodian refuses a new key release without a finalized, unexpired break-glass authorization for this responder and record. The responder app no longer holds a universal emergency secret. | At least 1-2 days; changes the key-custody model. Strongest deeper privacy follow-up, not a last-minute patch. |

### 1. A receipt, not another dashboard

- Start with a read-only local verifier (CLI first if time is short). Give it a fixed network, known deployment and trusted read-only indexer. Do **not** take an indexer URL or trust anchor from the receipt. No signing wallet, private-state provider, IPFS access or new database is necessary.
- Export an allowlisted receipt: version, network, contract, finalized block hash, nullifier, policy commitment and amount in integer base units. The live adapter already receives a finalized call but [returns only its private result](../ui/src/zk/live.ts#L70-L84). Preserve the public block reference separately. **Never reuse the policy-card QR payload**: [it includes holder information, policy terms and salt](../records/src/card.ts#L20-L46). Reuse the QR renderer, not the data.
- The verifier reads `claims[nullifier]` **at that block** on the pinned contract and compares amount and policy. Give distinct results: `Matches ledger`, `Does not match`, `Cannot verify`. This establishes a recorded settlement, not an offline re-verification of a ZK proof. If you display a specific transaction or payee as verified, also check that successful `submitClaim` and its matching payout in that exact transaction. The settlement map alone does not store a payee or hospital root; the current UI [can use local metadata or the latest root](../ui/src/zk/demo.ts#L408-L415).
- Make the verifier a separate public-data-only entry point, not another role tab: [live mode keeps the demo identities together](../ui/src/zk/live.ts#L175-L193) and [caches hospital-side state](../ui/src/zk/live.ts#L245-L261). Inspect the verifier's actual network response. It must contain no name, diagnosis, procedure, prescription, record CID, card code, key or witness. Amount, policy linkage, timing and the unshielded payee are not anonymous.
- Release gate: genuine receipt matches in a fresh profile; altered amount or wrong deployment does not; unavailable indexer never shows a green result. The verifier trusts the configured local chain/indexer, not the browser that created the receipt. Use a second browser on the demo laptop: a QR pointing to `localhost` does not reach the laptop from a judge's phone.

### 2. Turn "yes" into a decision the patient still controls

- Today [`consentToClaim`](../contract/src/zkclaim.compact#L215-L226) only inserts a tag; [`submitClaim`](../contract/src/zkclaim.compact#L312-L323) checks membership. Doctor revocation is a separate action. Extend the existing consent circuit to grant/withdraw for that same exact record and policy, under the same ownership checks. Allow only a currently active authorization at settlement. Do not bolt a cancel flag onto the UI or a server queue.
- Keep the patient-facing notice short: who is asking, "settle this bill only", amount, what the insurer receives, what it does not receive, and when permission ends. Grant and withdraw should be equally easy. Put any named receipt on the patient side, not in a public event. If adding a deadline, bind it to the authorization and enforce it against chain time; otherwise claim withdrawal only, not expiry.
- Show `Withdrawal pending` until confirmed. **Ledger order decides:** if withdrawal finalizes first, even a claim prepared earlier must not pay; if settlement wins, show `Already paid`, never promise a refund. Check both orderings, an unauthorized caller, no extra payout, and a still-valid control claim. Re-run a real deployment before promoting it: [larger contracts have hit this node's block limit](architecture.md#engineering-note-deploy-size-and-block-limits).

### Where the government angle is real

- **Consent:** India's [DPDP Act, sections 5 and 6](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf) addresses notice, specified purpose and comparably easy withdrawal where processing relies on consent. The [commencement notification](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/267647.pdf) phases these core provisions in 18 months after the November 2025 notification (May 2027). The [NHA's patient FAQ](https://abdm.gov.in/faq/abhamobileapp) describes granular, time-limited, revocable record sharing. Our proposed payment authorization borrows those design principles; neither source establishes a general legal right to cancel an insurance payout.
- **Interoperability:** the [NRCeS implementation guide, v6.5.0](https://nrces.in/ndhm/fhir/r4/6.5.0/hcx-profile.html) defines the NHCX claim and claim-response bundles. Today's [FHIR builder](../records/src/fhir.ts#L192-L210) makes a collection with a `Claim` and demo NIGHT amounts. That is not exchange conformance. For rank 3, map an adjudicated outcome into a *separate*, access-controlled synthetic response; keep any required patient references out of the public receipt. Use an explicit synthetic INR business amount, not NIGHT relabeled as INR. Save the validator version, profile, errors and warnings. Say "this sample validates against this profile" only if it does. No live NHCX submission, government endorsement, real ABHA identities or real rupee settlement.
- **Emergency access:** the [threat model](threat-model.md#residual-risks) explicitly says logging before decryption depends on the app and the current emergency key holder. For rank 4, remove that secret from all clients for newly sealed records; authenticate the responder and have a separate custodian check the current authorization before wrapping only the permitted section keys to them. This makes *new key releases* auditable under a trusted custodian; it cannot count offline rereads of keys/plaintext already obtained.

## The 90-second demonstration

| Time | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 0-15 s | Open on a hospital bill, not a blockchain diagram. Ask: **"If the policy limit is private, what stops the hospital making it up?"** Let the judge pick the existing altered-policy challenge. Show the contract's `Unknown policy` result. Label Tamper Lab **compiled circuit simulator**, not live proof generation. |
| 15-40 s | Switch to the real local deployment. Show the patient authorization and a successfully settled synthetic claim, with contract address and payout delta. Pre-fund and warm it; if proving takes longer than the slot, show an explicitly labeled earlier finalized claim from the same chain. |
| 40-65 s | Give the judge the receipt in a separate, wallet-free browser. They check it, change one digit of the expected amount, and check again. Show what actually came back over the wire instead of relying on black rectangles over secrets. |
| 65-90 s | If rank 2 shipped, let the judge withdraw on a **different, unpaid** claim; wait for confirmation, then show the contract refusal and unchanged paid total. Otherwise replay the already settled record and show the exact refusal and unchanged total. End here, not on an architecture slide. |

**Time cut:** Under two hours, keep the proven contract; do the state-only CLI receipt if possible, otherwise lead with the altered-policy challenge and the real payout. With two builders and an evening, one owns the verifier and one owns consent; keep the stable deployment until the new one passes. Freeze features at least an hour before judging. Record a short real run and measure prove-to-finality and receipt-check time on the demo machine; don't quote simulator timings as blockchain performance.

**Two rehearsal traps:** the [claim CLI](../cli/src/demo.ts#L146-L177) uses placeholder CIDs, labels `400` base units as `400 NIGHT`, and accepts any replay error as a rejection. Use the [UI unit convention](../ui/src/zk/service.ts#L231-L239), require the specific replay reason plus an unchanged total, and use the [access CLI](../cli/src/demo-access.ts#L125-L149) or the live Kubo path for storage evidence. Do not claim a replay nullifier prevents every duplicate real-world visit. Skip shielded payouts, a new chain, wallet sponsorship and an AI fraud score for this deadline: none makes the core claim as easy to verify.

**Validation for this plan:** 39 contract simulator tests passed; 37 records tests passed; 3 local Kubo integration tests skipped (node unavailable); UI typecheck passed. No new devnet settlement, real-wallet walkthrough, NHCX validation or legal assessment was performed for this review. Ranks 1-4 are proposals, not implemented features.
